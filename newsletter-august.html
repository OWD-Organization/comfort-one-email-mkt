Every Phoenix house has one room the AC just can't win.

You know the one. The back bedroom that's 8° warmer than the rest of the house. The garage you gave up on in May. The converted office over the garage that turns into a sauna by 2 PM. The casita nobody wants to sleep in until October.

Here's the thing most homeowners get wrong: cranking the thermostat down to 68° doesn't fix that room. It just makes your central system run harder, cool the rest of the house past comfortable, and hand you a bigger APS bill — while that one room stays hot.

This month we're talking about the fix that doesn't require tearing open walls or adding a single duct: ductless mini splits. Where they make sense, how the install actually goes, and the tips that separate a mini split that runs beautifully for 15 years from one that struggles by year three.

🏠 Where a mini split actually earns its keep

A mini split isn't a replacement for your whole-home system — it's a targeted fix for the spaces your ducts were never designed to reach. If any of these sound like your house, keep reading.

🚗
 The garage. An uninsulated Phoenix garage hits 110–120° in August — and everything in it pays the price: tools, paint, pantry overflow, that second fridge working itself to death. A mini split turns it into usable space year-round: gym, workshop, office, band room. It's the single most common install we do, and homeowners always say the same thing — "I should've done this five years ago."
🛌
 The bedroom that's always hot. Usually it's the one farthest from the air handler, on the west wall, at the end of a long duct run. A mini split gives that room its own thermostat — so one person can sleep at 68° without freezing out the rest of the house. This is the fix for "we argue about the thermostat every night."
🔨
 Additions, casitas & Arizona rooms. Adding square footage almost never comes with enough duct capacity to match. Instead of undersizing your whole house by stretching the existing system, a mini split conditions the new space independently — and doesn't put your main unit under load it wasn't sized for.
💻
 Home offices & rooms you use all day. If you're in one room from 8 to 5, you don't need to cool 2,400 sq ft to stay comfortable. Zoning that room separately lets you nudge the main thermostat up a few degrees during the workday — which is exactly where the savings come from.

🔧 What a clean install looks like

One indoor head, one outdoor condenser, one small line set through the wall. No ductwork, no drywall demo, no week-long project.

Most single-zone installs are done in one day — and the room is cold before we pull out of the driveway.

✅ 5 install tips that decide how long it lasts

Mini splits are simple machines that get installed badly all the time. Whether you hire us or someone else, these are the five things worth asking about before anyone drills a hole in your wall.

1
 Size it for the room, not for the biggest number. Oversizing is the #1 mini split mistake. A unit that's too big blasts cold, hits the setpoint in four minutes, and shuts off — never running long enough to pull humidity out. You end up with a room that's cold and clammy, plus a compressor short-cycling itself into an early grave. A proper load calculation (square footage, insulation, ceiling height, window exposure, sun orientation) beats a guess every time.
2
 Placement matters more than people think. The indoor head should be high on a wall with a clear, open path across the room — not tucked behind a door swing, above a bookshelf, or blowing straight into a wall six feet away. Outside, the condenser wants shade if you can give it (north or east side), a few feet of breathing room on all sides, and distance from bedroom windows. A condenser baking in full west sun works measurably harder every afternoon.
3
 Ask how they vacuum the line set. This is the boring step that separates a real install from a fast one. Before refrigerant goes in, the lines must be pulled into a deep vacuum to remove air and moisture. Skip it or rush it and you get acid forming inside the system, iced-up coils, and a compressor failure a few years out — usually after the labor warranty ends. If a bid is suspiciously cheap, this is often where the time was cut.
4
 In a garage, insulation is half the job. You can put a perfect mini split in an uninsulated garage and it'll still fight you all August. An insulated garage door, weather-stripped gaps, and sealed attic access do more for that room's comfort than an extra 6,000 BTUs. We'll tell you honestly what your space needs first — sometimes the smaller unit plus insulation is the better buy.
5
 Plan the condensate drain and the electrical up front. Every indoor head produces water, and it has to go somewhere with proper slope — not dribble down your stucco or pool behind the drywall. And most units want their own dedicated circuit. Sorting both of these before install day is the difference between a one-day job and a callback. Also worth confirming: that the installer pulls a permit and registers the manufacturer's warranty in your name.
