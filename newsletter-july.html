Your AC is running a marathon right now. Here's how to help it finish.

Phoenix family, we're in it. Triple digits every day, 115°+ on the ugly ones, and monsoon dust doing its thing at night. Your AC is running 20+ hours a day — the equivalent of a car engine idling around the clock, in the desert, with no breaks.

Most breakdowns in July aren't random. They're the result of small warning signs the homeowner missed in May and June. This newsletter is your peak-heat survival guide: four checks you can do this week, the signs your system is about to give up, and what to do if something feels off before it turns into a 2 AM emergency.

🌡️ The Peak-Heat Survival Checklist

Take five minutes today. Walk through your house with this checklist. If anything below sets off alarm bells, call us before it becomes a Saturday night crisis.

1
 Time how long it takes to hit temperature. If your AC used to catch up in 20 minutes and now runs for hours without hitting the setpoint, that's not the heat's fault — that's your system telling you something is failing (low refrigerant, dirty coils, or a compressor wearing out).
2
 Feel the air coming out of the vents. Healthy AC output is 15–20° colder than the room temperature. If your thermostat says 78° and the air coming out is 70°+, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem. Test with your hand or a cheap thermometer.
3
 Listen for new sounds. Grinding, hissing, high-pitched squealing, or rapid clicking that wasn't there in June means something is wearing out, leaking, or arcing electrically. New sounds in July almost always get worse — fast.
4
 Check your outdoor unit. Ice on the refrigerant lines, water pooling underneath, or the unit itself feeling scorching hot to the touch = red alert. Turn the system off and call us. Running an AC in that state can turn a $400 repair into a $4,000 compressor replacement.
